## 1. The event

### 1.1 Concept

> **Material to write from**
> - *Notte della Taranta Melbourne* is a **fictional** one-night festival at Flemington Racecourse — say so in the first paragraph, plainly. The app itself says so on the About screen.
> - It borrows its name and tradition from the real *La Notte della Taranta* in Melpignano, Salento. Acknowledge that explicitly; appearing not to know it exists is worse than borrowing from it.
> - The shape of the night is the argument: gates 18:00, first tamburello at dusk, three stages, closing at 04:00. **Terra Rossa** (acoustic and traditional) opens, **La Ronda** (main) carries the middle of the night, **Il Ragno** (electronic) runs midnight to dawn. The progression from traditional to electronic is the concept in miniature — the tradition handed forward rather than preserved under glass.
> - "La Ronda" is the circle dance; "Terra Rossa" the red earth of Puglia; "Il Ragno" the spider. The naming carries the narrative without explaining itself.
> - What a visitor actually does: arrives at dusk, can join a beginners' pizzica workshop without a partner or any experience, eats, moves between stages, and if they want to, steps into the circle. The ronda is participatory, not a performance watched from behind a barrier.

### 1.2 Why Melbourne, why Flemington

> **Material to write from**
> - **Melbourne** — a large Italian-descended population, a share of it from post-war emigration from the south including Puglia. ⚠ **This needs an ABS Census citation or it must come out.** The App's About screen makes the same claim and has the same problem.
> - **Flemington, practically** — it already handles crowds at this scale, has a dedicated railway station on the doorstep, and is a flat open site that can be made step-free throughout. For an event running to 04:00, transport that runs late matters more than atmosphere. That reasoning is on the About screen and is worth expanding here.
> - **Flemington, thematically** — a racecourse is a circuit, and the ronda is a circle. Worth a sentence if you believe it; cut it if it reads as reaching.
> - **The relocation itself** is the interesting problem. A ritual that answered specific conditions in rural Puglia is being staged in a southern-hemisphere city in late summer. That is not a neutral move and §1.4 is where you deal with it.

### 1.3 Cultural source material

> **Material to write from** — keep this factual and cited. See [06-References.md](06-References.md).
> - Tarantism: the bite of the *taranta*, real or symbolic, producing a state that only music was held to lift. Musicians called to the house, playing until the right rhythm was found, then continuing — sometimes for days — while the afflicted person danced.
> - The *tarantate* were **overwhelmingly women, and overwhelmingly poor**. Reading the bite as a purely medical event misses what the ritual actually carried: grief, labour, confinement, and the absence of any other permitted outlet.
> - Ernesto de Martino's 1959 expedition to the Salento is the standard ethnographic source. ⚠ Verify the date and the edition you cite.
> - The ritual as de Martino found it has largely gone. What exists now is a **revival** — festivals, recordings, staged pizzica — which keeps the music alive while detaching it from the crisis it once answered.
> - ⚠ **The Dionysian continuity claim is debated, not settled.** Hedge it explicitly. The app's chapter 1 sidesteps it by arguing structure rather than descent; you can do the same or make the claim and qualify it, but do not assert it flat.

`<write here>`

### 1.4 Cultural sensitivity

> **Material to write from** — this is the section that most rewards honesty, and it feeds Task 5's "social and cultural demands" theme directly.
> - The position the app takes, on story chapter 3: *"This app sits inside that contradiction rather than pretending it away. A festival is not a cure. Saying so plainly is the honest version of the tribute."* Decide whether you agree with that framing before you defend it.
> - What was done in practice, and can be pointed at: the three story chapters exist at all rather than the history being reduced to decoration; the About screen names the ritual's origins in poverty and gender rather than romanticising them; the app states it is fictional on its own About screen; the lineup is invented rather than putting words in real practitioners' mouths.
> - What is genuinely unresolved, and is worth admitting: a festival brand built on a healing ritual is extraction of a kind however carefully it is handled. Naming that tension is stronger than claiming to have solved it.
> - The spider mark with a heart-shaped body is a design decision with a meaning — it makes the *taranta* the emblem of the event. Say whether that was intended as affection, appropriation, or something more ambivalent.

## 2. Audience

### 2.1 Primary audiences

Filled from what the app actually provides. Check each against the built screens before submitting.

| Audience | Needs | How the app serves them |
|---|---|---|
| Local Melbourne attendees | Wayfinding, schedule, transport, tickets | Flemington map with stages and facilities; combined set-and-workshop timeline; Transport screen with train, tram, shuttle and parking; digital ticket with a scannable QR |
| Italian-Australian community | Cultural authenticity, language, connection to the source | Italian in the language list; three story chapters tracing the tradition; lineup grounded in Salentine instrumentation; merch drawn from Apulian objects — the *pumo*, the tamburello |
| International visitors | Language, context, orientation | Six languages shown in their own scripts; story chapters give the context a first-time visitor lacks; transport written for someone who does not know Melbourne |
| **Virtual visitors** | Remote access, and a reason to bother | Dedicated screen: live stream, stage switching, captions on every stage, **set times converted to the viewer's timezone**, 30-day replay, Virtual Pass at $25 |
| Families and older attendees | Accessibility, quiet space, timing, cost | Family craft workshop from age 6; quiet zone; seated versions taught alongside every dance workshop; three text sizes |
| Attendees with disability | Step-free routes, captions, sensory information, companion support | Step-free route drawn from station to main stage; Changing Places facility marked; accessible cubicles, parking and shuttles named; hearing loops at two stages; free Companion Card ticket; sensory warning for strobe and haze at Il Ragno; assistance animal relief area |

> The brief singles out **international and virtual visitors** in both Task 1 and the Expectations section. The Virtual screen and the language list are the direct answers — give them real weight in the writing.

### 2.2 What each audience needs from the app

> **Material to write from**
> - The strongest single argument: **nothing is gated behind an account**. Map, schedule, stories and music all work signed out; an account only syncs a ticket and saved sets. The Login screen says so on itself. A festival app that demands registration before showing you where the toilets are has misunderstood who it is for.
> - The second: **the accessibility provisions are not a settings screen bolted on**. They are distributed — access notes on every workshop, a step-free route on the map, captions defaulted on for the stream, sensory warnings where the strobes are. The settings screen is where the user takes control, not where accessibility lives.
> - The third: the app has to work for someone standing in a field at 2am with poor signal and one hand free. That drove the offline ticket, the drawn map rather than an embedded one, and the 44px minimum target size.

### 3.2 Considered and deferred

Good ideas, out of scope for a prototype. These are the future-direction section.

| Feature | Why it was deferred |
|---|---|
| Social feed / attendee posts | The nav is four items to match four icons; a fifth would break the consistency the brief marks. Also a moderation burden a student prototype cannot answer for |
| Push notifications — set reminders, "your stage starts in 10 minutes" | Genuinely useful and genuinely out of scope for a web prototype with no backend |
| AR pizzica tutorial — learn the step by following an overlay | The strongest future direction: it answers "how do I join in without embarrassing myself", which is the real barrier to a participatory ronda. Far beyond prototype scope |
| Offline caching of the whole app | The ticket is designed to work offline; caching the map and schedule too would need a service worker and a cache-invalidation story |
| Ticket transfer / group booking | Commerce depth beyond what the brief asks for |
| Live crowd density per stage | Needs sensors and a backend; would genuinely help accessibility planning |
| Personal schedule builder | The "Add to my night" button exists on workshops but does not persist a list. Half-built deliberately — it demonstrates the interaction without implying a feature that is not there |

### 3.3 Rejected

Decisions made and not revisited. Each one has a reason.

| Feature / approach | Why it was rejected |
|---|---|
| **Building the final design in Figma** | The brief permits other software. Figma would have given simulated interactions; code gives real scrolling, swiping and animation, and lets the accessibility settings actually change the running app. See [05-AI-Use-Log.md](05-AI-Use-Log.md) entries 8–9 |
| **Embedding a real map service** | Accurate and free, but needs attribution, breaks the visual identity the submission is marked on, and fails with no signal on a festival site. Drew original SVG artwork instead |
| **Photography on the artist profiles** | Every option was licensed, fake, or of a real person who is not in this festival. Used generated artwork from the campaign palette instead — honest about being invented |
| **Streaming real audio** | Every artist is invented, so there is nothing to stream. A silent transport that behaves correctly is more honest than autoplaying music that is not theirs. The Now Playing screen says so on itself |
| **The "Social" nav item** | It appeared on the About screen in the wireframe and nowhere else, and only four icons exist. Dropped rather than inventing a fifth icon and a screen behind it |
| **A pixel typeface for body copy** | Pixel fonts degrade badly under screen magnification, which works directly against the accessibility case Task 5 asks you to argue. Pixel type restricted to display and short labels; Atkinson Hyperlegible for everything read at length |
| **Press Start 2P as the display face** | Closest match to the wordmark's weight, but too wide — screen titles wrapped to three lines at 412px and it is illegible at label sizes. Silkscreen chosen instead |
| **A decorative QR on the ticket** | Would have looked right and failed the one thing the screen exists to do. Generated a real scannable code instead |
| **Colour-coded map pins** | Colour alone fails for a colourblind user. Pin type is carried by shape as well as colour, and every pin is also named in a list below the map |
| **A splash screen that requires a tap** | A door with no handle for anyone using a switch device. It auto-advances, with an Enter button as an option rather than a requirement |
| **Gating content behind login** | A festival app that demands an account before showing the map has misunderstood the situation it is used in |
| **Euro pricing** | Inherited from the wireframe's template boards. The festival is in Melbourne; everything is AUD |

## 4. Design approach

### 4.1 Identity carried from Assessment Task 2

> **Material to write from**
> - What is reused **verbatim**: the wordmark PNG itself appears on the splash, home and ticket screens, so brand consistency does not depend on matching a typeface. The five merch renders are the Task 2 artwork unmodified. The four pixel nav icons and the four section backgrounds are the campaign's.
> - What was **extended**: the palette needed mid-tones an identity system does not have — an app needs raised surfaces to separate a card from the ground. Three were derived by lifting the ground colour rather than introducing anything foreign.
> - The **alchemical sigils** baked into each section background now do navigational work: the sigil differs per section, so Home, Event and Music are distinguishable by texture before any text is read.
> - The **matrix-rain texture** returns as the splash animation, drifting behind the wordmark.
> - ⚠ One technical honesty point worth a line: the section artwork sits under a 78–88% scrim. That is not a design flourish — it is what guarantees the measured contrast ratios hold over the lighter magenta forms.

`<write here>`

### 4.2 Navigation model

> **Material to write from**
> - Four items — Home, Event, Music, Shop — matching the four icons that exist. The constraint came from the campaign, and it turned out to be the right number.
> - What lives where instead: **Settings** and **About** are reached from Home; **Ticket** and **Login** from Shop; **Map, Schedule, Transport and Virtual** from the Event hub. The hub pattern keeps the nav at four while giving the on-site features one obvious front door.
> - The wireframe's real defect was that the About screen showed five items and every other screen showed four. In the built app the nav is **one component rendered from one place**, so it cannot drift. That is a stronger answer than "we checked every screen".
> - Active state is carried by colour, a bar, *and* `aria-current` — never colour alone.

`<write here>`

### 4.3 The dark palette as an accessibility decision

> **Material to write from** — full measured table in [07-Contrast-Report.md](07-Contrast-Report.md).
> - It was a brand decision first. It happens to test extremely well: **white on `#040606` measures 20.31:1**, against a WCAG AA threshold of 4.5:1. Mint measures **11.99:1**. Every one of the 14 combinations in the app passes AA, and all but one pass AAA.
> - It suits the subject: a night festival, and a screen that will be looked at in the dark. Low glare at 2am is a usability argument, not just an aesthetic one.
> - The honest counterweight: **the campaign's magenta fails.** `#8B3A8B` on the ground measures **2.98:1**. Rather than treat that as a rule to remember, magenta was structurally excluded — there is no magenta text token in the stylesheet to reach for. It carries fill, border and texture only.
> - The single exception is the ticket's QR panel, which is white because a dark QR does not scan. Naming an exception and justifying it is stronger than claiming the system has none.
